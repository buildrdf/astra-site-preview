What Is a Kundali? Your Vedic Birth Chart Explained

A Kundali is a photograph of the sky, taken at the exact minute and place you were born.

The short answer

A Kundali, also spelled kundli, is a Vedic astrology birth chart — a fixed diagram recording the exact positions of the Sun, Moon and other grahas against the zodiac at the precise minute and place of your birth. It never changes; every later reading, dasha or transit in Vedic astrology is compared against this one fixed record.

A Moment in the Sky, Frozen in a Diagram

A Kundali is not a prediction. It is a record — the position of the Sun, Moon and every other graha at the exact minute you were born, seen from the exact place you were born. Change either the minute or the place and the chart changes, because the sky genuinely looked different from there, at that time.

Everything else in Vedic astrology — houses, dashas, transits, yogas — is a way of reading that one photograph. The sky keeps moving after you are born, every day, for the rest of your life. Your Kundali never does. It is the fixed reference the moving sky is measured against, which is exactly why it can be computed once and used forever.

The Three Ingredients Every Kundali Needs

Casting a Kundali needs exactly three pieces of information, and the chart is only as accurate as the least precise of the three:

  • Date of birth — fixes where the slower grahas (Jupiter, Saturn, Rahu, Ketu) sit; it barely moves within a single day
  • Time of birth — fixes the rising sign and every house cusp; it can change entirely within two hours
  • Place of birth — fixes which part of the sky was directly overhead, and therefore which sign was rising

This is why an approximate birth time — 'sometime in the morning' — is a real problem for chart accuracy, while an approximate birth date almost never is. The date barely moves the chart; the rising sign can flip entirely within a single hour.

Kundali, Horoscope, Birth Chart: Same Thing?

In everyday English, 'horoscope' usually means the short daily prediction printed under a Sun sign in a newspaper or app — twelve generic paragraphs, one per sign, with no birth time involved. A Kundali is a completely different, much more specific object: a full diagram computed from your own exact birth data, with your own houses, your own nine grahas, and your own nakshatras. When Indian astrology sources use 'horoscope' to mean the birth chart itself, they mean what this article calls a Kundali — not the newspaper column.

A generic Sun-sign horoscope and a real Kundali share almost nothing technically. One sorts the entire world's population into twelve buckets by birthday alone; the other is unique to your birth minute and place.

The Diamond, the Square, and What They Both Mean

Kundalis are drawn in a couple of different visual layouts across India, and this is purely a matter of convention, not calculation. The North Indian style — the one Astra draws — uses a fixed diamond, where house 1 always sits in the same spot and the signs are written into each house and rotate around it. The South Indian style uses a fixed square grid where each cell is always the same sign, and the houses rotate depending on your Ascendant instead. Read correctly, both diagrams describe the exact same chart; they are just two ways of drawing the same information.

Why Birth Time Matters So Much

Consider two children born in the same hospital, on the same date, three hours apart. Their slower grahas — Sun, Jupiter, Saturn — will sit in identical or nearly identical signs, because those move slowly enough that three hours makes no real difference. But the sky rotates roughly 15° every hour due to Earth's own spin, and the rising sign shifts about every two hours. Three hours apart is enough to put an entirely different sign on the eastern horizon at each birth — a different lagna, a different house 1, and from there, every single house in the chart falls in a different place. Two births, one date, two genuinely different Kundalis.

What a Kundali Does Not Tell You

A Kundali is not a fixed script of events. Classical Vedic astrology reads a chart as a set of tendencies, strengths and periods — traditionally associated with certain outcomes, never guaranteed to produce them. Two people can share an almost identical chart and still live very different lives; the chart describes a kind of terrain, not a fixed itinerary across it.

Be wary of any reading that states a specific future event as a certainty ('you will lose your job in March') rather than a traditional association ('this period is traditionally read as testing for career matters'). That distinction is the difference between astrology as it is classically practised and fear-based guessing.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between kundali and kundli?

Nothing — they are the same word, transliterated from Hindi/Sanskrit slightly differently. Kundli, kundali, janam kundli and janma patrika all refer to the same object: a Vedic astrology birth chart computed from your exact birth date, time and place.

Can I get an accurate kundali without knowing my exact birth time?

You can still see your Moon sign and the slower grahas' signs reasonably well without an exact time, since those barely shift within a day. But your Ascendant and all twelve houses depend directly on the minute of birth, so without it, the house placements and rising sign in your chart cannot be trusted.

Is a kundali the same as a horoscope?

Not the version most people know. A newspaper or app 'horoscope' is a generic daily message for an entire Sun sign, with no birth time involved. A kundali is a full chart unique to your own birth minute and place, with your own houses, grahas and nakshatras — a far more specific and detailed object.

Why do north indian and south indian kundali charts look different?

They are two visual conventions for showing identical information. The North Indian diamond keeps houses fixed in place and rotates the signs into them; the South Indian square keeps signs fixed in each cell and rotates the houses instead. Once you know which layout you are reading, both describe the same chart.

Does a kundali predict what will happen in my life?

Classical Vedic astrology treats a kundali as a map of tendencies, strengths and periods, traditionally associated with certain themes — not a fixed script. Two people with very similar charts can live quite different lives, so a kundali is read as a terrain to understand, not a guaranteed forecast of specific events.

What information do I need to generate my kundali?

Exactly three things: your date of birth, your time of birth as precisely as you can find it, and your place of birth. The place and time matter most, since they set your Ascendant and every house; the date mostly affects only the slower-moving grahas.

The Navagraha: The Nine Grahas of Vedic Astrology

Meet the cast: two luminaries, five visible planets, and two mathematical shadows.

The short answer

The navagraha are the nine grahas used in Vedic astrology: Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rahu, and Ketu. Graha means 'grasper,' not planet — the Sun and Moon are luminaries, and Rahu and Ketu are shadow points, the lunar nodes, with no physical body at all.

Why 'graha' and not 'planet'?

Every English summary of Vedic astrology reaches for the word planet, and every one of them is slightly wrong. The Sanskrit word is graha, and it was chosen deliberately. Graha means grasper — something that takes hold of a person's life — not a body orbiting the Sun. That is why the list includes the Sun and Moon, which are luminaries, and Rahu and Ketu, which are not physical objects at all.

The nine, in one line each

  • Sun (Surya) — the self, vitality, authority: where you must be visible
  • Moon (Chandra) — mind, mood, memory: how you actually feel
  • Mars (Mangala) — drive, courage, conflict: the will to cut through
  • Mercury (Budha) — intellect, speech, commerce: how you process and transmit
  • Jupiter (Guru) — wisdom, expansion, counsel: it enlarges what it touches
  • Venus (Shukra) — beauty, pleasure, partnership: what you are drawn toward
  • Saturn (Shani) — time, structure, endurance: it withholds, then rewards what survived
  • Rahu — the north lunar node: appetite without a natural limit
  • Ketu — the south lunar node: detachment, and the release of what Rahu grasps

Two luminaries, five planets, two shadows

The nine sort into three groups. The Sun and Moon are luminaries — the only bodies bright enough to matter to the naked eye that founded this system centuries before telescopes. Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn are the five planets visible without any instrument — the ones a person standing in a field at night could actually track. Rahu and Ketu are the odd pair: mathematical points, not bodies, and we will get to exactly what they are below.

Benefic and malefic are broad, traditional classifications, not verdicts. A malefic graha well placed does real, constructive work — Saturn's discipline and Mars's courage are both necessary. This natural quality is only the starting point; a graha's actual behavior in a chart depends heavily on the houses it owns and sits in, which is a separate lesson.

Rahu and Ketu, honestly

Rahu and Ketu are the lunar nodes — the two points where the Moon's orbital path crosses the Sun's apparent path (the ecliptic). Rahu marks where the Moon crosses heading north, Ketu where it crosses heading south. They are mathematical points, not bodies, which is exactly why eclipses happen there: an eclipse requires the Sun, Moon and Earth to align, and that alignment can only occur near a node.

Vedic tradition calls them chhaya grahas, shadow grahas, and treats them as a matched pair: always exactly opposite each other in the chart, and always retrograde, since the nodes themselves regress slowly backward around the zodiac. Because they have no physical mass, classical texts describe their effects as psychological and karmic rather than physical — Rahu as an amplifying hunger, Ketu as a habit of letting go, sometimes before a person feels ready to.

Not the Western nine

If you learned astrology through a Western or tropical source, you may expect Uranus, Neptune and Pluto on this list. They are not here, and that is not an oversight. Classical Vedic astrology — the texts this app follows, such as the Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra — was formalized long before those three were discovered with telescopes, and it uses these nine and no others.

A number of modern Vedic astrologers do experiment with the outer three planets as supplementary indicators, mostly for generational or collective themes rather than personal ones. That is a modern layer on top of the tradition, not part of the classical nine, and this app does not use it.

A graha is not a personality type

The single most common mistake with the navagraha is treating each one like a horoscope-column trait — 'Mars people are aggressive,' full stop. A graha's actual signal in your chart depends on which sign it sits in, which house that sign falls in, which houses it rules, and what aspects reach it. The one-line summaries above are starting points for a much longer conversation, not a finished reading.

How the nine start to combine

Once you can name the nine, the next question is where each one sits and what it looks toward — because a graha's placement, not its identity alone, is what a reading actually turns on. That is where houses, aspects and lordship come in, and each has its own grammar worth learning on its own.

Questions people ask

What does graha mean in Sanskrit?

Graha comes from a Sanskrit root meaning 'to seize' or 'grasp.' It was chosen instead of a word for 'planet' because the list includes the Sun and Moon, which are luminaries, and Rahu and Ketu, which are not physical bodies — a graha is defined by its influence on a life, not by its astronomy.

Why are Rahu and Ketu called shadow planets?

Rahu and Ketu are chhaya grahas, or shadow grahas, because they have no physical form. They are the two points where the Moon's orbit crosses the Sun's apparent path across the sky — mathematical intersections, not bodies — which is also why solar and lunar eclipses only happen near them.

Are Rahu and Ketu real astronomical objects?

Not as bodies. Rahu (the north node) and Ketu (the south node) are real, precisely calculable points in space where the Moon's path crosses the ecliptic, but they have no mass and cast no light. Their positions are astronomically exact even though nothing physical sits there.

Why doesn't Vedic astrology use Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto?

Classical Vedic astrology was codified before these three were discovered by telescope, and its foundational texts define the system around nine grahas. Some modern practitioners add the outer planets as supplementary, generational indicators, but they are not part of the classical navagraha and Astra does not use them.

Which grahas are considered benefic and which malefic?

Jupiter and Venus are traditionally natural benefics; Sun, Mars, Saturn, Rahu and Ketu are traditional malefics; the Moon is benefic when waxing. Mercury is neutral and traditionally takes on the nature of whatever graha it is closest to. This is a starting classification, not a fixed verdict on any single chart.

What is the difference between a graha and a rashi?

A graha is one of the nine influences — Sun, Moon, and so on. A rashi is one of the twelve zodiac signs a graha can sit in. Every graha occupies exactly one rashi at a given moment; the two are separate layers that combine to produce a placement.

How many grahas are there in total in Vedic astrology?

Classical Vedic astrology recognizes nine grahas, called the navagraha: Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rahu, and Ketu. Some regional traditions briefly mention additional minor points like Gulika, but the navagraha of nine is the standard, universally used set.

What Do Planetary Conjunctions Mean in Vedic Astrology?

Two planets, one sign, one blended story — and a clear order for deciding who leads it.

The short answer

A conjunction (yuti) happens when two or more planets share the same zodiac sign, so they occupy the same house and their significations blend. Vedic astrology reads conjunctions two ways — by shared sign (the structural reading) and by close degree (literal fusion) — and judges which planet leads mainly by dignity, then closeness, then what each one rules.

Contact in the Same Room

When two grahas (planets) occupy the same sign, they are conjunct — yuti. They sit in the same house, act on the same area of life, and their significations blend into one combined story rather than two separate ones. An aspect (drishti) reaches across the chart from a distance; a conjunction is contact in the same room, and it is the most direct relationship two planets can have.

Yuticonjunction — two or more grahas sharing one zodiac sign

Whole-Sign vs Close-Degree Conjunctions

Vedic astrology genuinely uses two different readings, and they answer two different questions. Whole-sign conjunction — simply sharing a rashi (sign), whatever the exact degree — is the structural reading: same house, same lordship consequences, the version meant whenever a chart summary calls two planets "conjunct." Degree conjunction asks how close the two actually are: two planets a few degrees apart blend far more literally than two sitting at opposite ends of the same thirty-degree sign.

Astra uses both, deliberately, for different jobs. A birth chart lists conjunctions by sign, because houses and lordships are counted in whole signs. A current-sky view flags a conjunction only when the two planets are astronomically close, within roughly three and a half degrees — near enough that you could step outside and actually see them together.

How Astrologers Decide Which Planet Leads

A conjunction is not an average of two planets; one voice usually leads. Tradition weighs this in roughly this order:

  • Dignity first — a planet in its own sign or exalted tends to colour the other rather than the reverse
  • Then closeness — a few degrees apart is genuine fusion; twenty degrees apart in the same sign is more a shared address than a shared voice
  • Then what each one owns — when two house lords meet in conjunction, their houses become linked, and that link is often the more useful fact than the conjunction itself
  • Then the specific pairing — Jupiter with Venus reads quite differently from Mars with Saturn, and classical texts carry a note on most common combinations

Graha Yuddha: When Two Planets Are Nearly Exact

Graha Yuddhaplanetary war — the classical name for two planets conjunct within about one degree of each other

Where two of the five visible planets (Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn) sit within roughly a single degree of each other, classical texts treat it as a contest and name a winner — judged by which appears brighter, by ecliptic latitude, or by exact degree, depending on the source. That disagreement between texts is old and unresolved, so Astra reports the closeness of such a pairing without scoring a winner.

Beyond Conjunction: Other Ways Planets Connect

Sambandhaa connection between two grahas — by sharing a sign, by aspect, or by exchanging signs

Conjunction is only one of the ways two planets connect. The others are mutual drishti (each aspecting the other), a rashi parivartana or sign exchange (each sitting in a sign the other rules), and simple lordship (one planet sitting in a house the other rules). Any of these links two parts of a chart together; a conjunction is simply the tightest and most literal of the four.

Worked Example: A Jupiter-Venus Conjunction

Suppose Jupiter sits at 10° Cancer, exalted, and Venus sits at 15° Cancer, neither exalted nor debilitated there. Both are natural benefics, so the pairing is traditionally read as generous and easy on its face — warmth, resources and goodwill meeting in one house. Because Jupiter is exalted and Venus is not, dignity says Jupiter leads: the house is coloured more by Jupiter's themes of growth, meaning and counsel than by Venus's themes of comfort and relationship, even though both contribute.

Getting Conjunctions Right

Common mistakes

  • Treating every conjunction as equally strong, ignoring how many degrees actually separate the two planets
  • Assuming a conjunction is a simple average, when one planet's dignity usually leads
  • Missing that a Sun conjunction may specifically be combustion, a distinct and named condition
  • Scoring a "winner" for every close conjunction, when only genuinely tight graha yuddha cases invite that reading, and even there texts disagree
  • Ignoring what each conjunct planet independently rules, which often matters more than the conjunction itself

Where traditions differ

Graha yuddha's winner is judged differently by different classical sources — by brightness, by ecliptic latitude, or by exact degree — and the schools do not agree. Some Western-influenced synastry approaches also read conjunctions purely by close degree and skip the whole-sign structural reading entirely; Vedic technique keeps both, because houses and lordships are counted by whole sign regardless of how tight the conjunction is.

Questions people ask

What does a planetary conjunction mean in Vedic astrology?

A conjunction, called yuti, happens when two or more planets occupy the same zodiac sign. They then sit in the same house and their significations blend into one combined story. It is the closest and most direct connection two planets can have in a chart, stronger than an aspect from a distance.

What is the difference between whole-sign and degree conjunctions?

Whole-sign conjunction simply means two planets share a sign, regardless of exact degree, and it drives house and lordship readings. Degree conjunction measures how close the two actually are in the sky; planets a few degrees apart blend far more literally than two at opposite ends of the same sign, even though both count as conjunct by sign.

How do you know which planet leads a conjunction?

Tradition checks dignity first — an exalted or own-sign planet tends to colour the pairing more than the other. Next comes closeness in degree, then what each planet independently rules, then the specific pairing itself, since some combinations (like Jupiter with Venus) carry their own classical notes distinct from either planet alone.

What is graha yuddha, or planetary war?

Graha yuddha is the classical term for two of the five visible planets (Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn) conjunct within about one degree of each other. Older texts name a winner using brightness, ecliptic latitude, or exact degree, but the criteria differ by source, so this remains a genuinely contested area of classical technique.

Is a Sun conjunction the same as combustion?

Not always. A planet conjunct the Sun by whole sign is a normal conjunction; it becomes combust specifically when close enough in degree to fall inside that planet's classical combustion orb, which ranges from about 8° for a retrograde Venus to 17° for Mars. Combustion is a named subset of Sun conjunctions, not every one of them.

What is sambandha in Vedic astrology?

Sambandha means a connection between two planets, and conjunction is only one form of it. The others are mutual aspect (drishti), a sign exchange where each planet sits in a sign the other rules (parivartana), and simple lordship, where one planet sits in a house the other rules. Any of these link two parts of a chart together.

Does a conjunction always mean the planets' effects mix equally?

No. A conjunction blends two planets' significations, but rarely as an even split. Dignity, closeness in degree, and what each planet rules usually determine which one leads the combined expression, so the same pairing can read quite differently across two different charts.

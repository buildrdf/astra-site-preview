The North Indian Chart: How the Kundali Diamond Works

One rule unlocks the whole diamond: houses never move, signs do.

The short answer

In a North Indian kundali, the top-centre diamond is always the 1st house and the houses run counter-clockwise from there in a fixed order. What changes chart to chart is which sign occupies each house, starting with the rising sign in house 1. Learn that one rule and the entire diamond becomes readable.

Houses fixed, signs rotate

The North Indian chart is a diamond of twelve cells, and one rule unlocks all of it: the houses never move, the signs do. The top-centre diamond is always the 1st house, no matter whose chart you are looking at. The houses then run counter-clockwise around the frame in a fixed sequence — only the sign numbers written inside each cell change from person to person.

Each cell carries a number from 1 to 12. That number names the sign occupying the house — 1 for Aries, 2 for Taurus, and on through 12 for Pisces. Whichever sign was rising on the eastern horizon at the exact moment of birth goes into the 1st house, and the rest of the signs follow it around the diamond in their natural zodiac order.

How to read any chart, step by step

  1. Find the top-centre diamond — that cell is always house 1, the house of the self
  2. Read the number written in it — that number is your rising sign, the lagna
  3. Count counter-clockwise around the diamond for houses 2 through 12
  4. Each cell's number tells you which sign sits in that house
  5. Any graha written inside a cell sits in that house, in that sign, together

A worked example: Leo rising

Suppose Leo, sign number 5, is rising at birth. Leo goes into house 1. From there, the remaining signs fill the other eleven houses in strict zodiac order — Virgo next, then Libra, and so on, wrapping back around past Pisces to Cancer just before returning to Leo. The easiest case to sanity-check the rule against is Aries rising. Because Aries is sign number 1, house 1 holds sign 1, house 2 holds sign 2, and so on all the way to house 12 holding sign 12 — house number and sign number match exactly. That is not a special rule for Aries; it is what happens whenever the rising sign and the house-numbering both start from the same point. Any other rising sign simply shifts the whole sequence by the same amount, as the Leo example above shows.

North Indian vs South Indian: different maps, same chart

If you have seen a Vedic chart drawn as a square grid instead of a diamond, you were looking at the South Indian style. There, the twelve signs sit in fixed positions on the grid — Aries is always the same cell, Taurus the next, and so on — and it is the marker for the lagna that moves from chart to chart instead of the house numbers. Both styles encode identical information; they are two ways of drawing the same underlying facts, not two different astrologies. Astra draws the North Indian diamond, so houses are fixed and sign numbers rotate.

What a filled cell actually tells you

When a graha's name or symbol appears inside a cell, it means that graha occupies both the house and the sign written in that cell at once. A Mercury drawn in the cell marked 6 in our Leo example sits in the 10th house (Capricorn) — read together as career and public standing, expressed through Capricorn's disciplined, patient signature, whatever else the chart says about it.

Common mistakes when reading a kundali

  • Reading the cell numbers as house positions in zodiac order, rather than as the sign occupying a fixed house
  • Assuming the top diamond is 'Aries' rather than 'house 1' — house 1 only becomes Aries if Aries happens to be rising
  • Miscounting clockwise instead of counter-clockwise when moving from house 1 to house 2
  • Confusing the North Indian diamond with the South Indian grid and expecting the same cell to mean the same house in both

A kundali is a snapshot for one exact moment and place, not a generic wheel. Two people born minutes apart in different cities can have different rising signs entirely, because the ascendant depends on both the time and the latitude and longitude of birth — which is why an accurate birth time matters more for this diagram than almost anything else in the chart.

Where this goes next

Reading the wheel is the mechanical half of the skill. The interpretive half begins once you ask what each sign's ruling graha is doing elsewhere in the chart — that is house lordship, and it is where a kundali stops being a diagram and starts being a set of sentences about a life.

Questions people ask

How do you read a North Indian style chart?

Start at the top-centre diamond, which is always house 1. Read the number written there for your rising sign, then count counter-clockwise around the diamond for houses 2 through 12. Every number in a cell names the sign occupying that house; any graha written in a cell occupies that house and sign together.

Why is the 1st house always at the top of a North Indian chart?

By convention, the North Indian diamond layout fixes house 1 at the top-centre cell for every chart, regardless of whose birth details are used. What changes between charts is which sign number is written inside that fixed cell — the house position never moves, only the sign occupying it does.

What is the difference between the North Indian and South Indian chart styles?

In the North Indian diamond, houses are fixed in position and the sign numbers rotate to match the rising sign. In the South Indian square grid, signs are fixed in position and a marker for the ascendant moves instead. Both represent identical astrological information in different visual layouts.

What does the lagna mean in a Vedic chart?

Lagna is the ascendant — the zodiac sign that was rising on the eastern horizon at the exact moment and place of birth. It is placed in the 1st house of the chart and represents the self, the body, and how a person meets the world.

Do the houses in a Vedic chart move clockwise or counter-clockwise?

In the North Indian diamond layout, houses run counter-clockwise starting from the top-centre 1st house. This is a fixed convention of the diagram itself, separate from the actual motion of any graha or sign in the sky.

What does it mean when a planet is written in a chart cell?

It means that graha occupies both the house and the sign printed in that cell at the same time. For example, a graha in a cell marked '10' sits in Taurus and in whichever house Taurus occupies for that particular birth chart.

Vimshottari Dasha: How Vedic Astrology Answers 'When'

A chart shows what's there. The dasha system says when it switches on.

The short answer

Vimshottari dasha is Vedic astrology's main timing system: a fixed 120-year cycle divided among the nine grahas — Ketu (7), Venus (20), Sun (6), Moon (10), Mars (7), Rahu (18), Jupiter (16), Saturn (19), Mercury (17). Your Moon's birth nakshatra picks which graha's period you're born into and how much of it remains.

What a Chart Shows vs. What a Dasha Tells You

A birth chart is a snapshot: it shows which grahas own which houses, where each one sits, and how strong or weak it is. None of that says when any of it becomes active in a life. That's the job of a dasha system — a timetable that names which graha is 'running' at any given moment. The main one in Vedic astrology, used by Astra, is Vimshottari dasha, a fixed 120-year cycle.

The Vimshottari Sequence: 120 Years, Nine Grahas, One Fixed Order

Vimshottari divides 120 years among the nine grahas in a fixed order and fixed proportions that never change from person to person — only the starting point differs, as covered below.

How Your First Dasha Is Chosen: the Birth Nakshatra

Where you enter this 120-year cycle is not random and not tied to your birthday — it's set entirely by your Moon's nakshatra at birth. Each of the 27 nakshatras has a ruling graha, assigned from the same nine-graha cycle, and the lord of your janma nakshatra (birth star) runs your very first mahadasha. Because you were born partway through that nakshatra rather than at its exact start, only a fraction of that first period is actually left to run — the rest is treated as already 'spent' before birth.

How to Calculate a Dasha Balance: A Worked Example

The balance of the first dasha is the unelapsed fraction of the birth nakshatra, multiplied by its lord's total years. Here's a clean worked example.

  1. Suppose the Moon at birth sits at exactly 3°20' of Aries — that is, 3°20' of sidereal longitude
  2. Ashwini, the first nakshatra, spans 0° to 13°20' — the Moon is 3°20' into a 13°20'-wide nakshatra, i.e. exactly one quarter (one pada) through it
  3. Elapsed fraction = 3°20' ÷ 13°20' = 0.25 (25% of Ashwini has already passed); the remaining, unelapsed fraction is 0.75
  4. Ashwini's lord is Ketu, whose full mahadasha is 7 years
  5. Balance at birth = 7 years × 0.75 = 5.25 years — 5 years and 3 months of Ketu mahadasha remain from the moment of birth

Every subsequent mahadasha after that first, partial one runs its full length from the table above, in the fixed nine-graha order, cycling back to the start (Ketu) once all nine have run — 120 years is one full lap, and the sequence simply continues for anyone who lives past it.

Antardashas: The Sub-Periods Inside Every Mahadasha

Each mahadasha subdivides into nine antardashas — same nine grahas, same order, same proportions, just scaled down to fit inside the mahadasha's own length. A Jupiter mahadasha, for instance, contains a Jupiter antardasha, then a Saturn antardasha, then Mercury, and so on through the same sequence, each sized as (mahadasha length) × (that graha's years ÷ 120). Antardashas subdivide again into pratyantardashas, so the texture of a life's timing shifts every year or two rather than staying fixed for an entire mahadasha.

  • A mahadasha sets the broad theme for years at a time
  • Its antardashas shift that theme every one to a few years
  • Pratyantardashas shift it again, month to month
  • The graha starting each mahadasha's antardasha sequence is always that same mahadasha's own lord

What 'Ruling' a Period Actually Means

A graha ruling your current period does not act from a generic script written for that planet. It acts from your chart — the houses it owns, the house it currently sits in, its dignity, and its aspects. The right question is never 'what is Saturn like,' but 'what is Saturn doing in this specific chart.' The same Saturn mahadasha plays out very differently for two different people, because it is amplifying two different natal placements, not delivering identical results to both.

Where Traditions Differ

Vimshottari is by far the most widely used dasha system and is Astra's default, but it isn't the only one. Classical texts also describe Ashtottari (a 108-year cycle), Yogini dasha, and several nakshatra-based or rashi-based alternatives, each with its own logic for sequencing and length. Different schools favour different systems, and cross-checking more than one is a common advanced technique — not a sign that one of them is 'wrong.'

Common Mistakes When Reading a Dasha

  • Judging a mahadasha by the graha's general reputation instead of by what it owns and where it sits in the actual chart
  • Forgetting that the very first mahadasha of a life is only a partial period, not a full one
  • Ignoring antardashas and pratyantardashas, which is where most of the month-to-month texture of a dasha actually lives
  • Assuming a dasha ruled by a 'good' graha guarantees ease, or one ruled by a 'difficult' graha guarantees hardship — dignity, house, and aspects decide that, not the graha's name alone

Questions people ask

What is a mahadasha in Vedic astrology?

A mahadasha is the major period in the Vimshottari system, presided over by one of the nine grahas for a fixed number of years — from 6 years for the Sun to 20 years for Venus. All nine mahadashas together add up to a 120-year cycle, run in a fixed order that starts from the lord of your birth nakshatra.

How is the first dasha at birth calculated?

It's calculated from the Moon's nakshatra at birth. The nakshatra's ruling graha runs the first mahadasha, and its remaining length equals that graha's total years multiplied by the unelapsed fraction of the nakshatra — how much of the nakshatra's 13°20' span was left when the Moon was born into it.

What order do the Vimshottari dashas run in?

Always this order: Ketu (7 years), Venus (20), Sun (6), Moon (10), Mars (7), Rahu (18), Jupiter (16), Saturn (19), Mercury (17), totaling 120 years, then repeating from Ketu again. Only the starting point — set by the birth nakshatra's lord — differs from person to person; the sequence and proportions never change.

What is the difference between a mahadasha and an antardasha?

A mahadasha is the major multi-year period ruled by one graha. Inside it, that period subdivides into nine antardashas — the same nine grahas in the same order and proportions, scaled to fit inside the mahadasha's length — which shift the texture of that period roughly every one to a few years.

Does a bad dasha mean bad things will happen?

No. A dasha's graha is read through what it owns and where it sits in that specific chart, not through a fixed script of good or bad outcomes attached to its name. The same mahadasha unfolds very differently for two different charts, because it is activating two different sets of houses, dignities, and aspects.

Are there dasha systems other than Vimshottari?

Yes. Classical Vedic astrology describes several alternative systems, including Ashtottari (a 108-year cycle) and Yogini dasha, among others. Vimshottari is by far the most widely used and is Astra's default, but different schools of astrology sometimes favour or cross-check against a different system.

The short answer

Steve Jobs was born 24 February 1955, 7:15 PM PST, San Francisco (Rodden rating AA, from his birth certificate). His Vedic chart shows Leo lagna at 29°04' — a knife-edge degree — Moon in Pisces in Uttara Bhadrapada, and Saturn exalted in Libra in the 3rd house.

A Chart You Can Actually Check

Search 'steve jobs kundli' and you'll find dozens of pages that already know the ending: he was a visionary, so his chart must show genius, and it does — because whichever chart is quoted is quietly matched backward to fit the story everyone already knows. That's not astrology, it's storytelling with extra steps. This page tries something different. It starts from a well-documented birth time, computes the actual chart from it, and reads that chart the way a serious student would — including the parts where the method has nothing useful to say, and the part where a different, equally tidy story was sitting one lagna away the whole time.

The birth data behind this chart carries a Rodden rating of AA — astrology's own scale for how trustworthy a birth time is, and the highest tier on it. AA means the time comes from an original document, in this case a birth certificate, not a magazine interview, a biography's rough guess, or a fan's assumption. Most celebrity charts circulating online carry no rating at all, which is exactly why they disagree with each other. This one is worth taking seriously precisely because the input is solid — not because the reading that follows should be taken as fact rather than tradition.

This is the chart computed by Astra's engine for that exact minute and place: sidereal positions on the Lahiri ayanamsa, houses laid out whole-sign from the lagna. Nothing on it is illustrated or approximated — it's the same orbital mechanics an observatory would use, cast for 7:15 PM PST on 24 February 1955 at 37.77°N, 122.42°W.

The Knife-Edge Lagna: Six Minutes From a Different Chart

Here is the single most important fact on this page, and it isn't a planet — it's a degree. At 7:15 PM, the lagna (Ascendant) sits at Leo 29°04'. That is a hair's width from the end of Leo. The rising sign advances roughly one degree every four minutes, so by 7:19 PM it has crept to Leo 29°53' — still Leo, but barely. Two minutes after that, at 7:21 PM, it crosses into Virgo entirely. A birth six minutes later than the AA-rated time on record, and every house in this chart belongs to a different sign.

This is not a rounding error you can shrug off. Whole-sign houses are assigned directly from the lagna's sign, so a Leo lagna versus a Virgo lagna doesn't nudge the chart — it reshuffles all twelve houses by one full sign, and hands the role of lagna lord, the planet said to represent the person themselves, to a completely different planet. This is exactly why a serious reader's first question about any chart, especially a famous one pulled from the internet, is not 'what house is Saturn in' but 'how good is the birth time, and what does the source say about it.'

Notice both stories in that last row sound completely convincing on their own. That's the whole lesson of a knife-edge chart: a plausible-sounding astrological narrative is easy to build around almost any lagna, which is exactly why the narrative alone proves nothing — only the accuracy of the birth time tells you which chart, if either, is worth reading at all. Several Steve Jobs kundli pages online do in fact use a Virgo ascendant; most never mention which source or rating their birth time relies on.

The Chart at a Glance

With the AA-rated time and Leo lagna established, here is what the rest of the chart shows — the everyday meaning first, in plain language, then the technical basis it rests on. None of these describe things Saturn or Mars 'did' to a life; classical Vedic astrology reads a placement as a traditional association, not a mechanism, and two people can share a placement and live entirely different lives.

Lagna Lord Sun in the 7th House

The planet standing in for the chart's own ascendant — the Sun — sits not in the 1st house of self, but in the 7th house of partnership, contracts, and the public-facing 'other.' Classical texts traditionally read this placement as a life where identity is worked out largely through partnerships, co-founders, and audiences, rather than in isolation — a chart-holder whose sense of self is bound up in one-to-one and public relationships rather than solitary pursuit.

Technically: the Sun, lord of the Leo lagna, occupies Aquarius 12°32' in the 7th house, in Shatabhisha nakshatra, pada 2. The lagna lord's house placement is one of the first things a Vedic reading checks, since it shows where the chart's central identity is 'spent.'

Exalted Saturn in the 3rd House

Saturn sitting in its sign of exaltation is traditionally read as one of the strongest placements a chart can carry for discipline, structure, and long, patient effort that eventually gets recognised. In the 3rd house — the house of effort, communication, and one's own initiative — a traditional reader would note this as support for a relentless, almost obsessive attention to execution rather than raw inspiration alone.

Technically: Saturn sits at Libra 28°02', its sign of exaltation, in the 3rd house, in Vishakha nakshatra, pada 3. Exaltation is a dignity state — a measure of a graha's classical strength in a given sign — independent of which house that sign happens to fall in for a given lagna.

Mars in Its Own Sign, in the 9th House

Mars at home in its own sign is traditionally read as confident, decisive, unwilling to compromise on how a thing gets done. Placed in the 9th house — belief, philosophy, long journeys, the ideas a person builds a worldview around — a traditional reading would associate this with conviction that becomes almost doctrinal: not just having strong opinions, but building a whole outlook around them.

Technically: Mars occupies Aries 5°52', its own sign, in the 9th house, in Ashwini nakshatra, pada 2. A planet in its own sign is considered dignified regardless of house, though the house still colours what that strength is applied to.

Moon in Pisces, in the 8th House

The Moon — the mind, the emotional undercurrent — sits in Pisces, a sign traditionally associated with intuition, imagination, and a pull toward something beyond the purely practical. In the 8th house, the house of transformation, other people's resources, and sudden change, a traditional reader would note a temperament drawn to reinvention and to seeing further than the immediate moment, sometimes at real emotional cost.

Technically: the Moon is at Pisces 14°31' in the 8th house, in Uttara Bhadrapada nakshatra, pada 4. This nakshatra and its ruling planet also set the very first mahadasha of the life, covered in the timeline further down this page.

Venus and Rahu Together in the 5th House

Venus — aesthetics, refinement, what a person finds beautiful and wants to create — sitting alongside Rahu, the node traditionally read as amplifying and unconventional, in the 5th house of creativity and original ideas: a traditional reader would associate this with a hunger for a very particular, exacting kind of beauty applied to invention itself, rather than to decoration.

Technically: Venus sits at Sagittarius 27°57' and Rahu at Sagittarius 10°11', both in the 5th house — Venus in Uttara Ashadha nakshatra pada 1, Rahu in Mula nakshatra pada 4. A conjunction like this is read together, each graha's classical significations blending with the other's.

Retrograde Jupiter With Ketu in the 11th House

Jupiter, expansion and broad vision, appears retrograde — traditionally read as an inward, unconventional, often more intense version of that planet's usual expansiveness — sitting with Ketu, the node of detachment and letting go, in the 11th house of networks, gains, and large communities of people. A traditional reader would note this as a relationship to large audiences and big outcomes that isn't purely acquisitive: a wanting of scale paired with an odd indifference to holding on to any one piece of it.

Technically: Jupiter, retrograde, sits at Gemini 27°11' in Punarvasu nakshatra pada 3; Ketu sits at Gemini 10°11' in Ardra nakshatra pada 2 — both in the 11th house.

The Dasha Timeline Next to the Public Story

Vimshottari dasha is Vedic astrology's timing layer — a fixed sequence of planetary periods that runs across a whole life, starting from the Moon's birth nakshatra. Jobs was born into a Saturn mahadasha (his Moon's nakshatra, Uttara Bhadrapada, is ruled by Saturn), with about 3.07 years of it left to run. What follows is the full mahadasha sequence for this chart, laid next to the public events named in his biography. Read it as an exercise in a real technique, not as proof the technique 'worked' — a long, eventful public life will always have something happening in every multi-year window, which is precisely why matching a chart to a life after the fact is easy, and why it proves very little on its own.

A few things are worth naming plainly rather than dressing up. The Saturn and Mercury mahadashas together span birth to 1975 — more than twenty years before the first event on the public record this page uses, so there is nothing to line up here at all, and that's an honest result, not an inconvenient one. Apple's founding in April 1976 falls inside the Ketu mahadasha that began the year before; a traditional reader could note Ketu's classical association with detachment and unconventional paths and call that fitting, but millions of charts run a Ketu mahadasha at some point without founding a company in a garage, so the fit says more about human pattern-matching than about Ketu.

The most instructive stretch is the twenty-year Venus mahadasha, March 1982 to March 2002. It contains Jobs leaving Apple and founding NeXT in September 1985 and buying Pixar's graphics group in 1986 — by most accounts his most public professional setback — and it also contains Apple's acquisition of NeXT in December 1996, his return as interim CEO in September 1997, and the 1998 iMac. One mahadasha, one ruling graha, and two opposite chapters of the same career. If a single planet's period could reliably forecast good fortune or bad, it could not contain both of those. The iPhone's introduction in January 2007 falls in the following Sun mahadasha — notable mainly because the Sun is this chart's own lagna lord, which a traditional reader might connect to a period of the self, rather than a partnership or a network, taking centre stage. He died in October 2011, during the Moon mahadasha that began in 2008; that fact is stated here purely as a point on the timeline, not as something this chart or any dasha is being used to explain.

Sade Sati, 1993–2000: The Return, Not the Ruin

Sade Sati is the roughly seven-and-a-half-year stretch when Saturn transits the signs immediately before, upon, and after a person's natal Moon sign — for a Pisces Moon, that's Saturn moving through Aquarius, Pisces, and Aries. Popular astrology treats it as something close to a curse: a guaranteed period of hardship, best survived rather than lived. This chart is a clean way to test that reputation against a documented life, because it has two Sade Sati windows to look at.

The first window, January 1964 to April 1971, covers Jobs' childhood and early teenage years — a period this page has no documented public events to test it against at all. That absence is itself worth sitting with: not every Sade Sati window maps onto anything notable, for him or for anyone, and a method that only ever gets tested against its hits was never really being tested. The second window, March 1993 to June 2000, is far more interesting, because it runs straight through Apple's acquisition of NeXT in December 1996, Jobs' return as interim CEO in September 1997, and the launch of the iMac in 1998 — widely regarded as the beginning of Apple's modern era. If Sade Sati reliably meant ruin, this is not the seven years you'd expect to find it in.

One chart cannot prove Sade Sati is harmless any more than a difficult chart could prove it's ruinous — a sample of one settles nothing either way. What it can do is puncture the flattest version of the folklore: 'Sade Sati equals disaster' is simply too blunt a rule to survive contact with an actual, well-documented life. The honest position is the same one Vedic tradition itself takes at its best — Saturn's transit through these signs is traditionally read as a period of restructuring and hard-won maturity, which can arrive as loss, as a demanding comeback, or as both in sequence, exactly as it did here.

What This Chart Cannot Tell You

Astra does not read any chart, this one included, for illness or death. Jobs' death in October 2011 is noted on this page only as a fact of the timeline — it is not linked here to any placement, dasha, or transit, and a serious astrological reading never should be. Beyond that, there are broader limits worth naming honestly:

  • This chart cannot tell you Jobs would build Apple — it can only be read that way after the fact, which is a very different and much weaker claim than prediction
  • Millions of people share a Leo lagna, a Pisces Moon, or an exalted Saturn without becoming a household name; a placement describes a tendency, not a guaranteed outcome, let alone a specific one
  • The 'Virgo lagna' comparison earlier on this page is proof that a different, equally tidy story was available from a birth time only six minutes off — which means the story-fit itself is not strong evidence for either chart
  • Nothing here should be read as certainty about the future of anyone's life, Jobs' included; classical astrology at its most careful speaks in traditional associations, not guarantees

How to Read Your Own Chart the Same Way

The method used on this page works on any chart, including yours. It's the same five moves, repeated:

  1. Check your birth time's quality first — the exact minute, from a birth certificate or hospital record, not a family guess; an uncertain time near a house or sign boundary can flip your whole chart the way six minutes flips this one
  2. Find your lagna and its ruling planet, then see which house that ruler sits in — that's the closest thing a chart has to a 'main character' placement
  3. Go planet by planet and note two things for each: its dignity (own sign, exalted, debilitated, or neither) and the house it occupies
  4. Find your current mahadasha and antardasha, then ask what that planet rules and where it sits in your own chart — not what the planet is 'like' in general
  5. For every reading, swap 'this placement means' for 'classical texts traditionally associate this placement with' — if a sentence about your chart states a specific future event as a certainty, that's a signal to be skeptical of it, not to act on it

Questions people ask

What was Steve Jobs' ascendant (lagna)?

Leo, at 29°04' — using the AA-rated birth time of 7:15 PM PST on 24 February 1955 in San Francisco. This is an unusually late degree of Leo, close enough to the boundary with Virgo that a birth time only six minutes later would place the ascendant in Virgo instead.

What was Steve Jobs' Moon sign in Vedic astrology?

Pisces, at 14°31', in the 8th house, in Uttara Bhadrapada nakshatra, pada 4. This is also the nakshatra whose ruling planet, Saturn, governed his first mahadasha at birth.

Why do different websites show different birth charts for Steve Jobs?

Mostly because of small differences in the recorded birth time, and sometimes a different ayanamsa (the offset used to convert tropical to sidereal positions). Because his ascendant sits at a knife-edge degree of Leo, even a few minutes' difference in birth time is enough to push it into Virgo, which reshuffles every house in the chart. This page uses the Rodden AA-rated time from Astro-Databank, sourced from his birth certificate.

Was Steve Jobs in Sade Sati when he returned to Apple?

Yes. His second Sade Sati window, when Saturn transited the signs around his natal Pisces Moon, ran from March 1993 to June 2000 — covering Apple's acquisition of NeXT in December 1996, his return as interim CEO in September 1997, and the 1998 iMac. It's a useful case against treating Sade Sati as automatic misfortune, though one chart cannot settle the question either way.

Did Steve Jobs have an exalted planet in his chart?

Yes. Saturn sits at Libra 28°02', its sign of exaltation, placed in the 3rd house. Exaltation is one of the strongest classical dignity states a planet can have.

Does this chart mean Steve Jobs' success was written in the stars?

No, and no serious reading of it should claim that. A chart can be read after the fact to find placements that sound consistent with a known life — that's easy to do with almost any well-documented life and any chart. It cannot be used, before the fact, to predict that kind of outcome, and Vedic astrology's own traditional language is built around associations and tendencies, not guarantees.

Does Steve Jobs' chart explain his illness or death?

No. Astra does not read charts for death or disease, for Steve Jobs or for anyone else, and this page does not connect his death in October 2011 to any placement or planetary period. It is mentioned only as a date on the timeline.

What is a Rodden rating, and why does it matter here?

It's a scale astrologers use to grade how reliable a recorded birth time is, from AA (from an original document like a birth certificate) down to speculative guesses. Steve Jobs' birth time carries an AA rating, sourced from Astro-Databank, which is why this page treats the resulting chart as worth reading closely — most celebrity charts online carry no stated rating at all.
